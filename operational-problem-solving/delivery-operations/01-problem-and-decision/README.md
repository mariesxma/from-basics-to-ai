# 01 — Define the decision

**Format:** worked learning case with exercises. All organisations, data, and examples are fictional.

## Start with the situation

A fictional dispatcher has ten minutes before the morning review to choose which two orders to investigate. They can contact a warehouse lead or carrier coordinator, but cannot create extra delivery capacity.

These timings and limits are explicit exercise assumptions. They are not interview findings.

## Worked output: problem brief v1

- **User:** morning dispatcher.
- **Decision:** which two orders require investigation now, and who should investigate each one?
- **Problem hypothesis:** fragmented information makes urgent, actionable orders difficult to identify. This may contribute to delays; causation has not been established.
- **Workflow today:** read order sheet → inspect warehouse updates → check carrier messages → contact an owner → record follow-up.
- **Scope:** one dispatch review at 09:00 UTC on 5 October 2026, using six synthetic orders.
- **Constraint:** two investigation slots; an unresolved data problem must not silently look safe.
- **Out of scope:** automatic rerouting, customer promises, staffing changes, and automated messages.
- **Success proposal:** reduce time to choose and assign valid investigations without increasing unnecessary escalations. Delivery punctuality is a later outcome to examine.

## Questions before using this in reality

Ask operators whether two slots is realistic, whether “delivered” means arrival or handover, who owns each exception, and how often data refreshes. Ask for recent examples that contradict the prioritisation hypothesis.

中文：先定义谁做什么决定，而不是先决定做仪表盘。这里的时间和容量都是练习假设。

## Your task

Change one assumption, such as investigation capacity, and explain which later decisions it would affect. Keep a list separating supplied facts, assumptions, and open questions.
